ABSTRACT  
This work reports a measured temperature profile along the pump light beam inside the Er
3 +
:Yb
3 +
: LuAl
3
(BO
3
)
4 
crystal and the simulated ones. The temperature profile featured by a high and asymmetrical peak structure 
shows rather localized nature with respect to its transversal distribution. By scrutinous examination of the dis -
crepancies between the experimental and simulated ones, a) a heat deposition mechanism elucidating the steady 
state of local heat generations and various heat transfers was suggested; b) a three-beam of lights model together 
with a formula for analyzing the fractional thermal load was developed, which leads to a good agreement be -
tween the simulated and experimental temperature profiles. The new formula provides a more transparent tool in 
physics to evaluate the various energy losses; The model can easily be adapted to solve the thermal problem in a 
laser medium, in particular when a medium show unignorable absorption effect for the laser signal.   
1. Introduction 
Diode-pumped solid-state lasers in the end-pumped configuration 
own the advantages of high efficiency, compactness, and reliability 
[1,2] . In the design of such lasers, a careful consideration must be taken 
to mitigate the thermal effects such as thermooptic aberrations, lensing 
and birefringence, which lower the efficiency of laser operation, reduce 
the output beam quality and change the resonating condition [3 – 5] . The 
heat generation can be caused by quantum defects [6,7] , stimulated 
emission [8] , energy transfer upconversion [9,10] , excited-state ab -
sorption [11] , cross-relaxation [12] , and fluorescence process [7] etc. In 
its extreme case, if extrinsic defects and/or quantum defects [3] exist, 
these effects may become serious, which lead to local stress fracture, 
melting, and other damages to the laser crystal. Thus, the thermal 
management has long been of critical importance in the design of laser 
systems. In previous experimental studies, usually only the surface 
temperatures were measured by using infrared camera [5,13] . While a 
recent study made an important progress in measuring the temperature 
distribution within a crystal [14] . This makes it possible to study 
quantitatively the roles of various factors involved in the heat genera -
tion process, if theoretical simulations can be done to scrutinize the 
various models and assumptions. 
In principle, the pump power, the absorption coefficients, the 
dispersion of the light beam, the presence of defects on the light path and 
other parameters as well as their temperature dependence, if any, may 
influence the actual temperature distribution within a crystal. Due to the 
complexity of the problem, model analysis approach has been tried from 
the beginning up to the recent time to unravel it [15 – 17] . In the last few 
years, with the development of computational technique, a number of 
numerical simulations have been used to study the temperature distri -
butions within the laser crystals [2,5,18 – 23] . While these investigations 
obtained qualitative knowledge of the various factors such as pump 
power, absorption coefficient, the radius of light beam waist and cooling 
conditions etc. in governing the temperature distribution 
[2,5,18,20 – 23] , the quantitative knowledge have remained elusive due 
to the lack of experimental data.

2. Experimental method and simulation models 
2.1. Experimental conditions 
A temperature profile along the pump laser beam through the Er:Yb: 
LuAB crystal was measured by using the fluorescence intensity ratio 
technique [14] with the experimental setup shown in Fig. 1 . A contin -
uous wave laser of 976 nm generated by a fiber-coupled diode laser with 
a 100 μ m core diameter was used as a pumping source. The pumping 
beam was focused by using a telescopic lens system into the laser crystal, 
and had a beam waist diameter of 160 μ m. An uncoated Er:Yb:LuAB 
crystal (5 × 5 × 0.74 mm
3
) and a closely contacted 2.4 mm thick sap -
phire plate were mounted on a copper holder cooled with water of ~ 
293 K. A film deposited onto the front surface of the sapphire as an input 
mirror has a transmissivity of 90 % at 976 nm and a reflectivity of 99.8 % 
at 1.55 μ m. An output mirror with a curvature radius of 100 mm and a 
transmissivity of 1.7 % at 1.55 μ m was used. The cavity length was 
adjustable around 100 mm. The UC fluorescence signal emitting from 
the 5 × 0.74 mm
2 
surface of the crystal was first focused by using a 
convex lens with a focal length of 27 mm and then filtered to remove the 
scattering signals from the pump light and the laser. The image and the 
clean fluorescence signals were measured with a point-by-point scan 
mode by using a high-resolution optical fiber spectrometer (QE6500, 
Ocean Optics), while the crystal was in a resonating operation for a 1.55 
μ m laser.

The heat generation and deposition mechanisms in a gain medium 
can be rather complicated. In principle, if the photons decay into an 
appreciable number of phonons (multi-phonon relaxation [24] ) through 
various processes such as fluorescence [7] , up-conversion [9,10] , 
excited state absorption [11] , cross-relaxation [12] , concentration 
quenching [7] etc., heat results. Such processes may involve the re -
laxations either from the pump band to the upper metastable level or 
from the lower laser level to the ground state, or from the dead sites or 
from other defects, etc. Among these relaxations, the processes involving 
the laser levels are generally considered as the main source of heating 
[3,25] . In contrast to the heat generation mechanisms, the deposition 
mechanism has less been noticed. The traditional thinking emphasizes 
the role of thermal conductivity of a host material, which does not take 
into account the microscopic details of the heat transport [26] . This 
work used a phenomenological steady-state heat equation as follows to 
probe the temperature distribution in the crystal.

3. Results and discussion 
3.1. Experimental results 
Shown in Fig. 3 is one measured temperature profile (Exp-SCC) along 
the beam path in the crystal with an incident pump power of P
in 
= 5.43 
W. It is obvious that the lowest temperatures (~427.0 – 412.0 K) occur 
at the two ends of the beam path, while in the intermediate a broad peak 
centered at z = ~ 0.24 mm appears. This general feature has also been 
observed in previous theoretical simulations [2,21,23] , however, not yet 
well understood. In particular, if one takes a close look at the experi -
mental curve, one can find a strong asymmetry of the big blunt peak and 
a possible dip at its summit together with a low and broad peak occur -
ring at the end of the right tail. In order to optimize the experimental 
conditions, three independent measurements with different parameters 
have also been made. To understand the observed experimental features 
quantitatively, simulations based on different models together with in- 
depth analysis have been performed. Here, we analyze only the results 
for the first measurement, the analyses for the rest three are given in 
SM5.

To explain the observed temperature distribution, we first consider a

and b) are the transversal and longitudinal temperature distributions

3.3. Simulation vs. experiment: Minor features of the temperature profile 
3.3.1. Minor features beyond one-beam model: The role of laser beam 
parameters 
Our discussions in the previous section focus on the main features of 
the temperature profiles. As one can see from Fig. 4 c, the simulated 
curve for SCC (Sim-SCC) differs from the experimental one (Exp-SCC) in

part of these features has appeared in earlier simulation curves 
[2,21,23] , however, due to either the lack of experimental data or the 
neglect of these features, the implications of these features have never 
been explored, which leads to an oversimplified one-beam model and 
exaggerated fractional thermal load coefficient etc.

4. Conclusion 
Our studies of temperature distribution within the Er:Yb: LuAB 
crystal by using experimental measurements together with sophisticated 
theoretical simulations have revealed a very localized character of the 
heat generation along the light path within the crystal, which is so 
serious that the crystal can easily be damaged if no good cooling mea -
sures were taken. A heat deposition mechanism was proposed to 
describe the steady state of heat generation and heat transfers. The 
observed peak structure and large asymmetry of the temperature profile 
on the two sides of the peak were shown to be the result of the decaying 
pump energy due to the absorption, and the very different cooling 
conditions created by the sapphire and air. By scrutinous comparison of 
the simulated temperature profile with the experimental one, we pro -
posed a three-beam of lights model which takes into account the thermal 
effects of incident pump light, its reflected residue after absorption and 
the laser within the resonance cavity. The latter two components were 
completely ignored in previous works. A formula of fractional thermal 
load was derived based on the analysis of various possible energy pro -
cesses within the crystal, with which a reasonable value of the parameter 
was obtained. Our study indicates that though the absorption of the 1.55 
μ m laser by the crystal is indeed small, its thermal effect cannot be 
neglected. The possible effect of impurities was excluded by our simu -
lations with designed impurity containing models. The influence of the 
temperature dependence of the absorption coefficient was shown to be 
too small to explain the Disc-a. The simulated temperature profile with 
the three-beam of lights model and better parameters is in rather good 
agreement with the experimental one. Our study provides a practical 
approach to analyzing the thermal effect within an optical crystal, and 
suggests important factors needed to be considered in designing a laser. 
The developed approach can also be applied to evaluate a laser medium 
regarding to its thermal behavior, possible impurities and self- 
absorption of the laser signals.
